Campaign Desk Video selection Thu 30 July · two days out

What video would Enid watch?

No video is provided and the prizes are still to be announced, so the choice is entirely ours — which makes it the highest-leverage decision on the table.

Checked first

The event page provides no video, no dataset and no sample corpus, and says “prize categories and judging details are still to come”. Organisers are still confirming final links and access. We bring our own material.

Earnings calls. Because credit analysts already watch them — and nobody has ever checked what was said against what was filed.

A lender’s job is partly listening. Management describes leverage, earnings and adjustments out loud, on camera, weeks before any certificate is signed. That spoken record is a primary source in credit, it is entirely unstructured, and it is audited by nobody.

Why this is the answer

Three sources, one number, and they disagree

TwelveLabs reads on-screen text through OCR as well as speech and audio. That is the fact that makes this work, and I did not expect it. It means one video carries two independent claims about the same figure — what the slide shows and what the person says — and Enid already holds the third.

SourceHow we read itClaimFigure
The slide behind them Visual · OCR “Net leverage 4.2×” 4.20×
What the CFO says Transcription “comfortably inside our covenant” ~4.2×
What the documents require Enid Amendment No. 2 capped the add-back and stepped the limit to 4.00× 4.48×

Nobody is lying. That is the point worth making to a judge — and worth making honestly. Discrepancies like this are almost always innocent: rounding, a different basis, a definition that changed after the deck was built. Which is exactly why an agent that surfaces them for a human beats one that accuses anybody of anything.

The watchable moment

The call is playing. A flat voice cuts across it: “The slide says four point two. He said comfortably inside. The agreement says four point four eight, against a limit of four.”

Carried on sound, over the video the judge is already watching — which is the channel this desk has actually won on, and one almost nobody at a video hackathon will use. It is not a graph drawing itself, which the desk refuses as a chart redrawing.

Why a set, not a single video

The graph has to earn its keep

One video does not need a graph — it needs a transcript. The graph only justifies itself when it answers something no single clip can. So we index four calls: the same borrower across consecutive quarters, or two borrowers in the same portfolio.

  • “Every time this company described adjusted earnings, what number did they give and when did the definition quietly change?”

    Needs claims from four videos linked to one metric, ordered in time. Unanswerable from any one of them.

  • “Who was speaking when the leverage guidance moved, and what was on the slide behind them?”

    Links a person, a scene, an OCR reading and a spoken claim — four entity types in one hop.

  • “Which of our borrowers used the phrase ‘run-rate synergies’ this quarter?”

    The portfolio-wide question a credit desk actually asks, across companies rather than within one.

The graph shape

Person— said → Claim— about → Metric— for → Period— governed by → Covenant

With Scene hanging off every claim — prepared remarks or Q&A, and the timecode — so any assertion in the graph can be played back at the second it was made. That playback is what makes it demonstrable rather than merely asserted.

The catch that could cost the day

Video has to be indexed before it can be queried, and indexing is not instant. With a 3h30m build window, discovering that a sixty-minute call takes twenty minutes to index — times four videos — is how the build ends with nothing on screen.

Index everything on Wednesday. If pre-built work is disallowed we throw the index away and re-run it, having lost nothing but knowing exactly how long it takes. That number is worth more than anything else we could learn before the doors open.
