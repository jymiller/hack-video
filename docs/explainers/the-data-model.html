Admitting evidence nobody vouched for

A credit graph is built for documents somebody is accountable for. News footage is not one of those. This is how the hackathon graph lets it in without letting it lie.

Scope

What this is, and is not

Built entirely from public sources — broadcast clips, the bond prospectus, filed accounts, and matters of court record. Everything on this page can be checked by a stranger.

Deliberately excluded

Nothing here reproduces Enid's internal schema, its vocabularies, or its trust-grading scheme. The use case and the business thinking are shared by agreement; the mechanisms are not, and are not on this page.

An earlier draft did contain internal material and was removed before any commit.

The question

Not "video in a graph"

Credit systems are built around a controlled document supply chain — things the lender is sent. A signed agreement. Audited accounts. A compliance certificate. Every one arrives from a known party who is accountable if it is wrong.

News footage is none of those things. Nobody sent it. Nobody is on the hook for it. It was not produced for the lender and carries no obligation to be right.

How does a credit graph admit external, uncontrolled observation without letting it contaminate the record?

That is the contribution. It is worth building because the answer is not obvious, and because a system that has only ever consumed controlled documents has never had to answer it.

Why bother

Observed evidence is early

The controlled document is authoritative and late. Accounts reflecting the runway decision arrive many months after the decision. The broadcast is unauthoritative and immediate — it exists the same afternoon.

Sep 2025 · approval on the news → Jun 2026 · court ruling on the news → … months … → accounts reflecting the capex

The lead time is the product. An observed signal cannot change a covenant test. It can tell you which covenant test to go and look at, months before the document that settles it exists.

The rule, and it is enforceable

An observed source can never produce a Fact.

It produces only an Observation.

An Observation may corroborate, contradict, or flag a Fact. It may never become one, and it may never be what a covenant test reads. The integrity check returns zero rows or the graph is wrong.

Structure

Nodes

Segment is separate from Source on purpose. An Observation evidenced by "Channel 4 News, somewhere" is worthless. One evidenced by "Channel 4 News at 4:31–4:38" seeks the playhead to the proof. The timecode is the product.

NodeWhat it isKey properties
SourceOne retrievable thing provenance_class, kind, url, publisher, published_at, sha256
SegmentA timecoded span of video video_id, start, end, transcript
ObservationAn assertion from an observed source text, value, unit, currency, confidence, modality
FactAn assertion from a controlled source value, unit, currency, as_of, effective_date
ConceptWhat is measured or named name, aliases[], kind
EventSomething that happened on a date name, date, kind
CovenantA tested financial condition name, threshold, direction, test_dates[]
ExtractionRunOne model pass that produced nodes model, version, prompt_hash, tokens, cost_usd

Edges

Note the asymmetry at the bottom. An Event may affect a Covenant — a judgement, hedged, reversible. A Fact tests it — a computation. Different verbs because they carry different weight, and blurring them is how a demo starts lying.

// evidence
(:Segment)-[:PART_OF]->(:Source)
(:Observation)-[:CITES {start, end, bbox, modality}]->(:Segment)
(:Observation)-[:FROM]->(:Source)
(:Fact)-[:FROM]->(:Source)            // controlled only — enforced

// meaning
(:Observation|:Fact)-[:OF_CONCEPT]->(:Concept)
(:Observation)-[:CORROBORATES|CONTRADICTS {basis}]->(:Observation|:Fact)
(:Observation|:Fact)-[:ABOUT]->(:Event)

// consequence — note the two different verbs
(:Event)-[:MAY_AFFECT {direction, rationale}]->(:Covenant)   // judgement
(:Fact)-[:TESTS]->(:Covenant)                          // computation

// audit
(:Observation|:Fact)-[:PRODUCED_BY]->(:ExtractionRun)

Measured, not assumed

The model lies about modality

modality is the field the whole demo turns on, and it is the one the video model cannot be trusted to fill.

Observed on 28 July, on our own corpus

Asked to attribute each figure to speech or on-screen text, Pegasus labelled demonstrably spoken content as on-screen text — then asserted that nothing at all was spoken aloud in the clip. The search transcript proves otherwise.

Derive modality by cross-checking the search transcript against a separate OCR-focused prompt. Record confidence honestly. Never take one prompt's word for it.

The payoff

The query that makes the case

Three queries earn their place. The third is the demo.

// 1 · Where did this number come from, and may we believe it?
MATCH (o:Observation)-[c:CITES]->(s:Segment)-[:PART_OF]->(src:Source)
WHERE o.value = 14000
RETURN src.publisher, src.provenance_class, s.start, s.end, c.modality;

// 2 · What do our sources disagree about?
MATCH (a)-[x:CONTRADICTS]-(b) RETURN a.text, x.basis, b.text;

// 3 · What is the covenant actually tested on?
MATCH (f:Fact)-[:TESTS]->(c:Covenant), (f)-[:FROM]->(src:Source)
RETURN c.name, f.value, src.publisher, src.provenance_class;

Query 3 returns no observed sources at all

The water outage — the loudest, most recent, most watched event in the corpus, and the one on every news channel two days before we demo — has no path to a Covenant and simply does not appear. The graph excludes it by structure rather than by opinion. That is a stronger argument than any narration.

Decisions

What I need from you

None of this gets built until these are settled.

1 · Are two provenance classes enough?

controlled / observed is deliberately coarse so that nothing finer-grained is implied or exposed. If it is too coarse to be interesting, say so and we find a public-domain way to add resolution.

2 · Which concepts, and do any come across?

Six proposed above, invented for this corpus. You flagged concepts as possibly reusable — that is a decision to take on its own, and I have not acted on it.

3 · Which public filing supplies the covenant figures?

The prospectus, the supplement, or the filed accounts. All public, none gathered yet. This is the last real dependency before the graph can hold anything true.

4 · Who asserts MAY_AFFECT?

A human or a model. Leaning human — a model guessing at covenant impact in front of a judge is exactly the thing that should not be automated.

5 · Does Thursday's graph stand alone?

Standalone Neo4j, nothing written back anywhere. Recommended, and it keeps the whole question of what transfers for a calmer day than this one.
