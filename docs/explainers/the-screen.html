Actual build window

The agenda reads like a full day. It isn’t. Hacking opens at 11:00 and submissions are due at 16:00 — so by the desk’s own freeze arithmetic the binding stop is 14:30, not the 17:15 demo. Everything after that is subtract, submit and rehearse.

This is a two-gate event and that is unusual for us. A submitted artifact at 16:00 and a live demo at 17:15. The submission gate binds first, so it sets the freeze — 14:30 rather than the 15:15 the demo alone would give. Getting this wrong costs 45 minutes of slack, and slack is the strongest relationship in our whole record.

The shape of the gate

This is the configuration we win at

Live, in the room

1 – 0

Our single first place came standing in front of people, carried by a synthesised voice landing audibly in the room. Judges are present, they react, and sound works.

Submitted, nobody in the room

0 – 3

Three losses, and each time the under-investment was in exactly the artifact being judged. DataHub on 10 August is this shape — which is why it needed so much planning.

Thursday is mostly the left-hand column, with a 16:00 submission bolted to the front of it. That is genuinely good news: it is the surface we have evidence of winning on, and it rewards rehearsal over polish.

What they want built

Video in, graph out

The brief is unusually specific for a one-day event, which helps: ingest raw video, understand it across vision, audio and speech with TwelveLabs, and write what it finds into a Neo4j context graph — entities, scenes, tags, and the relationships between them.

raw video→ TwelveLabsvision · audio · speech→ entities · scenes · tags→ Neo4j graph→ agent asks it questions
What carries over from the DataHub work

More than you would expect. We have just spent a day thinking hard about context graphs — entities, relationships, and an agent that answers a question by traversing them rather than by guessing. That is the same shape of problem with a different source: video frames instead of financial tables, Neo4j instead of DataHub.

The reusable part is the thinking, not the code: what makes a graph worth building is that it answers a question a flat reading cannot. On Thursday the equivalent question is something like “what happened between these two people across three videos?” — unanswerable by watching any one clip, trivial once the graph exists.
