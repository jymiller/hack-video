# The corpus — Gatwick

*Proposed, not settled. See [`../STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md).*

## Why Gatwick

The DataHub work produced a demonstration corpus built around **Meridian Cold Storage
Holdings** — a company that does not exist. It was generated by one of Campaign Desk's
crews: a fictional $250m term loan, two fictional amendments, a fictional CFO signing a
fictional compliance certificate that is wrong twice over.

The design is sound. For video it is useless: **a judge cannot verify a word of it**, and
there is no footage of a company that never existed.

**Gatwick is the same story except it actually happened, and the lenders actually had to
waive the covenants.**

## The credit structure, and it is public

| | |
|---|---|
| Listed secured bonds via Gatwick Funding Ltd | ~£2.5bn, maturities 2024–2050 |
| Senior interest cover ratio, year ended 31 Mar 2018 | 3.59× — down from 3.96× |
| Covenants | Senior ICR and Senior RAR (debt ratio) |
| **August 2021** | **Lenders waived both, on £2.8bn of reference net debt, at the Dec 2021 and Jun 2022 test points** |

Gatwick is financed the way infrastructure credit is financed, its covenants are disclosed,
and there is a documented, public, real covenant event to point at. That is exactly what
Enid exists to see coming, and it gives the demo an ending that can be checked.

## The two events, and the trap

| | The drone | The pandemic |
|---|---|---|
| When | 19–21 December 2018 | From March 2020 |
| Flights cancelled | ~1,000 | — |
| Passengers affected | ~140,000 | Traffic collapsed |
| Closure | ~33–36 hours | — |
| Cost to airlines | ~£50m | — |
| **Cost to Gatwick itself** | **~£1.4m** | **~£2bn pre-tax loss for 2020** |
| **Covenant outcome** | **None** | **Senior ICR and Senior RAR waived** |
| Television coverage | Wall-to-wall, worldwide | Diffuse, months of it |

**Obvious reading:** the airport was shut down by drones and it was on every news channel on
earth, so that is the credit event.

**Right reading:** the drone cost Gatwick about £1.4m and moved nothing. The pandemic cost
about £2bn and its lenders had to waive the covenants.

A system that reacts to the loudest footage gets this backwards. One that follows the
footage through to passenger volumes, then earnings, then the ratio the bonds are tested
on, gets it right. That is the argument for a context graph rather than a transcript — and
unlike Meridian, every number is public.

## What is in the Internet Archive

Searched live on 28 July with the `ia` CLI, not assumed.

- **894 items** match Gatwick in the film collections
- `BBCNEWS_20190417_023000_The_Gatwick_Drone_Attack` — a BBC documentary on the incident
- `BBCNEWS_20190419_023000_The_Gatwick_Drone_Attack` — second broadcast
- `ALJAZ_20181220_190000` → `ALJAZ_20181222_100000` — Al Jazeera's rolling coverage across
  the three days of the shutdown, hour by hour as it happened

**Two notes on dates.** Gatwick has been a commercial airport since 1958 and an aerodrome
since the 1930s, so historical newsreel of it exists. But the Internet Archive's
*television news* collection begins around 2009 — which comfortably covers both the 2018
drone and the 2020 pandemic, the only two events that moved the credit. The long historical
arc is available but it is decoration; the credit story sits entirely inside the archive's
strongest window.

## The demo shape

```
news footage → TwelveLabs → event · place · date · figures → Neo4j graph
             → passenger volumes → earnings → Senior ICR
```

- **Transcription** gives what the reporter says
- **OCR** gives the figures on the lower third and the studio graphics
- **Vision** gives the scene — a terminal full of stranded passengers reads differently
  from a press conference

All three write into one graph. The graph connects a Thursday night in December to a ratio
in a bond covenant, and answers the question no single clip can:
**"which of these events actually threatened the debt?"**

### The watchable moment, as proposed

Drone footage playing, chaos in the terminal. A flat voice cuts in:

> *"One thousand flights cancelled. One hundred and forty thousand passengers. Cost to the
> airport: one point four million. Interest cover unaffected. This is not the one."*

Then the graph jumps to March 2020.

Carried on sound over footage the judge is already watching, saying something
counter-intuitive inside the first fifteen seconds. It is not a graph drawing itself, which
this desk refuses as a chart redrawing.

**Open:** whether opening on the drone and overturning it is worth the seconds it costs. It
is the strongest three minutes available and also the most complicated.

## Reusable at the DataHub event

If Gatwick works on Thursday it should replace Meridian in the 10 August DataHub entry as
well. One corpus, two hackathons, and the weakest part of that plan — a fictional borrower
nobody can check — becomes its strongest. The DataHub story survives the swap intact: a
waiver or an amendment changes what a ratio means, and lineage says which covenant tests
depend on it.
